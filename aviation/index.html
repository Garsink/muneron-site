Deividas Jurkstas · founder's account

We built it to help with the decisions in our own business.

I run an aviation parts, MRO and landing gear leasing group. HAI began with that work: technical records to review, incoming enquiries to answer and assets to assess before we committed money.

This is not the €390 package specification. The connected aviation work described here uses internal systems and role-specific implementations. The public self-service pack is still in development.

From records to a decision

Our back-to-birth records workflow organises the documents, maps component histories and prepares findings for an engineer to assess. Commercial work brings the opportunity, outstanding questions and potential costs into the same decision process. Reading the files is one part of it; the technical judgement and buying decision still belong to people.

That is the connection to MUNERON. The aim is to prepare the work so the next person can decide, without having to reconstruct where every statement came from.

Then it had to work at somebody else's desk

A colleague working with airline customers in Asia Pacific has a role-specific setup bringing fleet information, available units and her contacts together. It helps her identify relevant operators and prepare offers for her own review and sending.

That setup is connected to internal data through a role-limited bridge. Some information is absent from the data she receives, rather than merely accompanied by an instruction not to mention it. Those permissions and connections are part of the internal implementation, not a universal feature of a downloadable folder.

The smaller first MUNERON pack focuses on customer enquiries and approved documents. It does not include the records engine, fleet data, mailbox access or an AvSight connection.

What the internal evidence says

As of 9 September 2026, the development record describes four colleagues using role-specific setups on Mac and Windows. There is not yet a published track record of independent customer installations.

A stop control needs a defined reach

A useful stop procedure names which jobs it covers, who can operate it and how someone knows it has taken effect. Its behaviour when a check is unavailable must be tested, not assumed.

What carries over

A named role, deliberately limited access, approval before consequential actions, source-backed outputs and a way to check the setup again. These are the principles being turned into the Desk Pack.

Integrations, technical record reviews and commercial decisions remain separate work. In aviation, the relevant technical and operational people still assess the findings and make the decisions.
