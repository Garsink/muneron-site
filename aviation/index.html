Internal aviation example

Where the checks
came from.

Before a Desk Pack, there was a business to run: incoming requests, technical records, draft offers and decisions that still belonged to people.

This is not the €390 package specification. The connected aviation work described here uses internal systems and role-specific implementations. The public self-service pack is still in development.

One desk, connected to its work

The internal quoting workflow brings incoming RFQs together with stock and quote history. It prepares a prioritised queue and draft responses, with price exceptions held for review. People decide what goes out.

The earlier implementation scope named AvSight, marketplace feeds such as ILS and PartsBase, historical RFQ calibration and a shadow run in which staff assessed the drafts. Those are integration and delivery tasks, not something a generic folder installs into every business.

The broader plan described a 90-day implementation, named responsibilities and staged acceptance. That custom project scope is separate from the planned self-service Desk Pack; it is not a timeline or service commitment attached to €390.

What the internal evidence says

As of 9 September 2026, the development record describes four colleagues using role-specific setups on Mac and Windows. There is not yet a published track record of independent customer installations.

A stop control needs a defined reach

A useful stop procedure names which jobs it covers, who can operate it and how someone knows it has taken effect. Its behaviour when a check is unavailable must be tested, not assumed.

What carries over

A named role, deliberately limited access, approval before consequential actions, source-backed outputs and a way to check the setup again. These are the principles being turned into the Desk Pack.

Integrations, technical record reviews and commercial decisions remain separate work. In aviation, the relevant technical and operational people still assess the findings and make the decisions.
