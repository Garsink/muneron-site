The system

Four months of operating AI.

The operating layer was built inside an aviation parts, MRO and landing gear leasing group. Over four months it reached 1,650 commits across 15 repositories through written instructions and review, without a developer typing code. It began with one question about the company and expanded function by function as operating requirements emerged.

Timeline.

  1. April
    Knowledge base. Company mail, chat and documents made searchable in one place.
  2. May
    Core functions. Enquiry handling, quote drafting, onboarding paperwork, live reads of the ERP and the accounts, a decision queue for the owner.
  3. 31 May
    Governance. A registry of every running job, a gate on outbound email, written operating rules. Added three weeks after the first live sends.
  4. June
    Quality gates. A sanity check on every draft, alerts when a data feed stops, a supervisor over the functions.
  5. July
    Stop controls and census. One master stop over every job that can act. A census of all jobs, with stale ones retired. An automatic-send pilot under explicit grants. Regression evaluations.
  6. August
    Hardening. A fail-closed stop rule, a dedicated customer-facing machine, the records function rebuilt, employee starter packages, operating rules reconciled with the running system.
  7. 2 Sep
    Adversarial audit. 311 evaluations across all four machines.
  8. 20 Sep
    Manual re-verification. 20 audit findings re-checked by hand against the original logs.

How it is wired.

Nine inputs, one operating layer, six recipient groups, four outbound channels.

  1. Connect signals before reporting them. Related items are reported together, with their relationship stated.
  2. Route to the process owner. Only items that need a decision are sent, and only to the person who owns that process.
  3. Verify against the system of record. No figure is shown unless it was pulled from the source at the time of writing. Wrong data is flagged and routed for correction, not used.
  4. Deliver to a named owner. Every recommendation has a recipient. Actions follow propose, approve, execute, confirm.

Functions.

The person owning each process decides. The layer reads, drafts, checks and chases.

Enquiries and quotes

Read incoming requests, price them against stock and history, and draft quotes. Approved offers can send automatically under set rules, with manual-only control for any customer.

Purchasing

Track what needs buying, request supplier offers, and write the offers back into the system so nothing is asked twice.

Finance

Maintain a live view of cash, receivables and payables. Every figure is pulled fresh from its source, with bank data taking precedence over book records.

People

Handle staff questions and paperwork. Health and personal data are never routed by job title.

Market

Read market and regional sources each morning and report only the items that changed, with the source next to each.

Meetings

Convert recordings into minutes and track the follow-ups. Attendance is taken from the recording, not the invitation.

Marketing

Extract publishable signals from internal meetings, with figures and names removed and a verification note on each item.

Knowledge

Keep an append-only log of mail, chat, meetings and deals, separated into lanes so restricted material never reaches a shared summary.

Controls

Verify recipients, apply the lowest permission in any group, screen payment language, keep the ERP read-only by default, and audit the layer's own output.

Outbound channels.

The layer reads widely and acts outward through four channels. Each is authorised by the owner, gated, logged and revocable.

  1. Internal email. Capped per recipient per day and in total, screened for confidential content, listed in a daily digest.
  2. Customer quotes. Enabled only while an explicit arming file is present. A price floor applies to every quote. Any account can be set to manual only.
  3. Marketplace submissions. Separate stop control.
  4. Public stock listings. Explicit arming, daily cap, separate pause.
One master stop covers all four channels.

Two fixed rules: the layer does not sign documents and does not move money. Reports to the owner are never gated, so a stop is always reported.

Controls, built in.

Recipient verification

A message must name its recipient in two independent places, and both must match, or it is not sent.

Scope gate

Outbound drafts are classified, every recipient including copies is checked, and the lowest permission in the group applies.

Payment language

Messages containing payment or bank instructions are held unless explicitly released, and every decision is logged.

Figure freshness

A financial figure is re-pulled from its source when the draft is written. A stale source is a refusal.

ERP access

Read-only by default. Only approved quoting functions write, to their own records, through one audited path.

Self-audit

Monitoring over the monitors, an adversarial audit of every job, and manual re-verification of a sample of findings.

Audit record.

On 2 September an adversarial audit evaluated 311 jobs across all four machines. It found 102 cases where a job finished without error and reported success while its output was wrong or reached nobody. The largest sources were not model errors: a control verified on the wrong machine, a processing chain that continued on schedule after its input feed stopped, and operating rules that no longer matched the running system. A first repair wave was applied the same week.

On 20 September, 20 of the 102 findings were selected at random and re-verified by hand against the original logs. 19 held. 1 was wrong: the audit had read two lines marked blocked and reported that a daily cash alert never arrived, when the next line in the same file, 308 milliseconds later, recorded it as sent. Across the full log, 108 of 132 alerts were sent. The finding was corrected and the checker now reads the final delivery state before reporting on any send.

Scope: the layer was built for one company, against that company's systems. It is not offered as a downloadable product.
