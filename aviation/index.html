The system

The operating layer.

An AI operating layer built inside an aviation parts, MRO and landing gear leasing group. Four months, 1,650 commits across 15 repositories, added function by function as the business needed them.

How it is wired.

Nine inputs, one operating layer, six recipient groups, four outbound channels.

  1. Related signals are grouped. Items that belong together are reported in one place, with the relationship stated.
  2. Decision items go to the recorded process owner. Items that need no decision are not sent.
  3. Figures come from the named system of record. They are pulled when the report is created. When a source check fails, the item is withheld and marked for correction.
  4. Each recommendation has a recorded recipient. Actions are recorded as proposal, approval, execution and confirmation.

Functions.

Process owners keep approval. The layer reads source systems, prepares drafts, runs checks and sends follow-ups.

Enquiries and quotes

Read incoming requests, price them against stock and history, and draft quotes. Approved offers can send automatically under set rules, with manual-only control for any customer.

Purchasing

Track purchase requirements, request supplier offers, and record each offer in the system.

Finance

Read cash, receivable and payable balances from their source systems, with bank data taking precedence over book records.

People

Handle staff questions and paperwork. Health and personal data are never routed by job title.

Market

Read market and regional sources each morning and report only the items that changed, with the source next to each.

Meetings

Convert recordings into minutes and track the follow-ups. Attendance is taken from the recording, not the invitation.

Marketing

Extract publishable signals from internal meetings, with figures and names removed and a verification note on each item.

Knowledge

Store mail, chat, meeting and deal records in separate access-controlled logs, so restricted material is excluded from shared summaries.

Controls

Verify recipients, apply the lowest permission in any group, screen payment language, keep the ERP read-only by default, and audit the layer's own output.

Outbound channels.

The layer sends through four outbound channels. Each is authorised by the owner, gated, logged and revocable.

  1. Internal email. Capped per recipient per day and in total, screened for confidential content, listed in a daily digest.
  2. Customer quotes. Enabled only while an explicit arming file is present. A price floor applies to every quote. Any account can be set to manual only.
  3. Marketplace submissions. Separate stop control.
  4. Public stock listings. Explicit arming, daily cap, separate pause.
One master stop covers all four channels.

Two fixed rules: the layer does not sign documents and does not move money. Stop events report to the owner through a separate, ungated path.

Controls.

Recipient verification

A message must name its recipient in two independent places, and both must match, or it is not sent.

Scope gate

Outbound drafts are classified, every recipient including copies is checked, and the lowest permission in the group applies.

Payment language

Messages containing payment or bank instructions are held unless explicitly released, and every decision is logged.

Figure freshness

A financial figure is re-pulled from its source when the draft is written. If the source is stale, the figure is withheld.

ERP access

Read-only by default. Only approved quoting functions write, to their own records, through one audited path.

Audit record.

On 2 September an adversarial audit completed 311 evaluations covering the scheduled jobs on all four machines. It recorded 102 findings: jobs that finished without error and reported success while the output was wrong or reached nobody. The audit attributed the largest groups of findings to a control verified on the wrong machine, input feeds that stopped while processing continued on schedule, and operating rules that no longer matched the running system. The affected controls were changed during the week of 2 September.

On 20 September, 20 of the 102 findings were chosen at random and re-checked by hand against the original logs. 19 held. 1 was wrong: the audit had read two lines marked blocked and reported that a daily cash alert never arrived, when the next line in the same file, 308 milliseconds later, recorded it as sent. Across the full log, 108 of 132 alerts went out. The finding was corrected and the checker now reads the final delivery state before reporting on any send.
