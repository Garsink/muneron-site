For companies using AI as a chat today

From a chat window to an operating layer.

Stop briefing your AI from scratch. In most companies every useful answer starts with one person re-explaining the business, and ends with that person. HAI is a digital company you can work with: it holds the company context, reads approved sources, remembers decisions, sets up agents for the work, and reports to the people who own each process.

This page is the practical map: what HAI offers today, what an operating layer is made of, what to have in place before day one, and the order to build in.

What HAI offers today.

Starter

A portable HAI that runs on a computer in your company with your own Claude account. It reads what you grant, builds the company context, briefs the owner on what matters and proposes where to go next. It does not send, write to your systems or move money. The right first step for a company at the chat stage.

Connected deployment

Your systems connected, agents and routines configured around your workflows and supervised, roles and permissions set for your team, and the actions you approve put behind rules and a stop switch. Scoped and agreed per company, workflow by workflow.

A useful answer is only the start.

A good chat gives you a good answer. The work begins after it: who checks the figures, who owns the next step, what happens tomorrow, and whether the same job gets done the same way next week.

What a chat does well

Reads four hundred emails and says which three matter. Drafts anything in seconds. Spots patterns people miss.

What it leaves to you

Checking the figures against your systems. Deciding who owns the outcome. Remembering what was decided. Doing it again next week.

What HAI adds

Shared company context, source checks on every figure, decisions that are retained, and agents and routines configured around your work and supervised. An owner for every output, and rules for anything that leaves the building.

The six parts.

Every operating layer, whatever the industry, is made of the same six parts. Missing any one of them is how a layer turns back into a chat.

Company context

Products, customers, suppliers, people and the company's own terms, held in one place HAI reads first.

Connections to approved sources

Mail, documents, accounting and the ERP, read-only and scoped to what you grant. Your systems stay the record.

Decisions and corrections that persist

What you decide and what you correct is written down and used next time.

Tailored agents and roles

Each job gets an agent or a routine configured around your sources and rules; each role gets its own view, with that role's permissions. Health and personal data never route by job title.

Routines and follow-up

Daily and weekly routines prepare, check and chase, and report what they could not do.

Permissions and verification

Every figure shows its source. Outbound actions exist only where you approve them, behind rules and a stop switch.

What one sentence produces.

An example from September 2026, with the company's name left out.

The owner of a food manufacturer, at the chat stage of AI, sent four files: two product catalogues and two slide decks. Asked for her biggest pains, she named three: daily KPIs, purchasing and cost, and information she could not find. That evening she had spent half an hour looking for her own company presentation and given up.

One sentence was given to HAI: review what she sent. It read the files, checked public sources, and by the end of the evening returned six documents: where to start on each of her topics, with an honesty label on every suggestion; the errors found in her own material, framed as checks before the next buyer pack; her company presentation redrafted from her own files, in her own style; the same presentation adapted for a retail chain; the architecture tour; and a three-minute film. The owner reviewed everything before it went out.

Her reply the next morning was one word long. What she asked for two days later was the list below.

Before day one: six things to have in place.

With these ready, the foundations go in quickly. Without them, every step waits on a missing answer.

  1. 1
    One internal owner. A person inside the company who will work with HAI every day and say when it is wrong. Ideally the business owner.
  2. 2
    A one-page company map. Units, people and responsibilities, main products, customer and supplier groups, the company's own terms. Hand-drawn is fine.
  3. 3
    A list of every system where data lives. Mail, files, accounting, stock, production, tills, the web shop. Next to each: who administers it, and whether the data can be exported.
  4. 4
    Access to the systems you select, a computer that is always on, and an AI account on a work address. The starter needs read access to what you choose to share. A connected deployment needs whoever administers each selected system available on the day.
  5. 5
    Red lines. What HAI may never do: send outside the company, touch money, read anything personal. And who approves what.
  6. 6
    One weekly routine from each of three to five key people. What they do now, what they want, what "done" looks like, and the spreadsheet they keep by hand. The first routines come from these.

The order to build in.

  1. First
    Company context. Mail, chat and documents made readable in one place. Context before anything else.
  2. Second
    The workflow that hurts most. For a trading business, incoming enquiries and quotes. For a manufacturer, likely purchasing and cost. One workflow, end to end, with a person approving the output.
  3. Same week
    Rules and permissions. A registry of every running routine, a gate on anything outbound, written rules for who approves what.
  4. Then
    Checks and follow-up. A sanity check on every draft, an alert when a source stops, routines that report what they could not do.
  5. Only then
    Actions. Where you approve them, with a floor, an opt-out, a log of every decision and a stop switch.

Four things owners assume.

"It needs programmers and a big IT project."
Every routine in the reference deployment started as a task described in plain words. What it needs is one owner who checks the output and says when it is wrong.
"It is there to replace people."
Same team, more handled. HAI takes the copy-paste half. People keep the judgment and the relationships.
"It reads everyone's private messages."
Company mailboxes, company systems and the work chats it is granted, all logged. No personal accounts, phones or private chats.
"AI is always right."
It makes mistakes, including confident ones. That is why every figure shows its source, people check its work, and the stop switch exists.
