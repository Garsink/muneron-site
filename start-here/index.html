From discovery to daily use.

The stages run around one useful first workflow: your team assesses real output before any wider rollout. Source access, the number of workflows and the depth of validation determine the schedule.

  1. 1

    Map the company

    Work with leadership and relevant process owners to understand the business, key handovers and recurring work. Identify where information is fragmented and what a useful first result looks like.

    Typical output: A company context map, priority workflows and named responsibilities.

  2. 2

    Review sources and access

    Inventory the selected systems, files and examples. Confirm who administers them, what can be connected and which material must remain restricted.

    Typical output: A source and access plan, with gaps and prerequisites made visible.

  3. 3

    Build the knowledge and rules

    Organise approved company context, working examples and instructions. Record important terms, decision responsibilities and what each role may use.

    Typical output: An initial knowledge foundation and readable operating rules.

  4. 4

    Configure the first workflow

    Agree the inputs, expected output, checks and review responsibility. Start with reading, analysis and preparation; scope connected actions separately where needed.

    Typical output: A working path from source material to an output your team can assess.

  5. 5

    Validate with the people who use it

    Check representative outputs against sources. Test access boundaries, incomplete data and the handover to the next responsible person. Record corrections and re-check the affected work.

    Typical output: Reviewed examples, a correction list and an agreed acceptance record.

  6. 6

    Hand over and develop

    Show nominated users how to ask, teach, correct and review. Agree support and upkeep, then decide which teams, routines or dedicated machines should follow.

    Typical output: A practical operating guide and a scoped expansion plan.

The initial scope and deliverables are agreed in the proposal. Additional departments, bespoke connections and dedicated machines are planned explicitly.

What we need from your team.

A practical starting point. You do not need to document the whole company before the first conversation.

  1. 1

    An internal sponsor and the right process owners

    Someone who can agree priorities, plus the people who know the selected workflows and can review the results.

  2. 2

    A rough map of the information

    The systems, files and communications used for those workflows, and who can authorise access.

  3. 3

    A few examples of good work

    A proposal, a report, a policy or another approved example that shows the quality and format you expect.

  4. 4

    Your boundaries

    Restricted sources, approval responsibilities and actions that must remain with people.

  5. 5

    A result worth checking

    One consequential question or recurring job, with a clear description of what a useful output would be.

A few practical questions.

How long does deployment take?

We agree a phased schedule after reviewing the workflows, sources and access requirements. A first useful workflow and a wider company rollout are different scopes. We make dependencies and acceptance criteria explicit before starting.

What comes with a supplied machine?

The HAI environment is prepared in advance. The proposal specifies hardware, account requirements, support, company-specific setup and what is handed over. Your authorised people complete sign-in and access approvals.

Can people improve it without programming?

They can provide examples, explain rules and review corrections in ordinary language. More involved integrations, access changes and recurring actions are handled as part of the agreed configuration.

We already use AI. What does HAI add?

A maintained company context, agreed working rules and a deployment shaped around your workflows. We look at what you already use and identify where shared knowledge, source checks and continuity would improve the work.

Does everyone need a dedicated machine?

No. HAI can begin on one supported computer. Additional role-based environments or machines are a scoped expansion option for workload and access separation, not a requirement for every employee.

What can HAI Seed do at the start?

The initial Seed environment reads approved information, analyses it and prepares answers and work for review. Company-system changes, outgoing messages and recurring connected actions require separately agreed configuration and validation.

Will HAI replace our existing systems?

Your mail, accounting, CRM and operational systems remain in place. We connect the supported sources or agreed exports needed for the work. The source systems remain the records to check.

How do you keep access appropriate?

We agree who may use each source and what each role may receive or do. Restricted material is scoped separately. Access and workflow checks are part of the deployment; local installation does not mean that AI processing is entirely offline.
