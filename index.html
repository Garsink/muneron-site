I typed my company an AI layer. Then I audited it.

Aviation parts, MRO and landing gear leasing. I am not a developer and I did not write the code. I described what I wanted, checked what came back, and described again.

It reads almost everything the company does, acts only inside rules I typed, and the big calls wait for me. This page is the record: what it does, and what it got wrong.

Tell me what you run ↗

The record.

  1. 219scheduled jobs running on four machines. Counted today, not quoted from a slide. Many of them exist only to watch the others.
  2. 311evaluations of those jobs in one adversarial audit, on 2 September.
  3. 102finished clean, showed green, and were either wrong or reached nobody. Not one had thrown an error.
  4. 20of those 102, picked at random, re-verified by hand against the original logs. Nineteen held. One did not.

The one I got wrong.

A vendor who never shows you their misses is showing you a brochure.

  1. What the audit said

    A daily cash alert was never reaching me.

  2. What it read

    Two lines in the log, both saying blocked.

  3. What the very next line said

    Sent. 308 milliseconds later, in the same file. Across the full log, 108 of those 132 alerts went out.

The finding was wrong, and it was wrong in the way that matters: it read a step in the reasoning as if it were the result. So it is published, and the rule that would have caught it is now in the checker.

The jobs are the smallest part of it.

  • It reads nine kinds of source. Every mailbox, the working chats, the ERP, the books and the bank feeds, the parts marketplaces, calendars and meetings, shared documents, fleet and market data, and its own past decisions.
  • One layer, four laws. Understand and connect. Choose and route. Verify against the live source. Close the loop to a real person.
  • Four lanes out of the building, one master brake. Email to colleagues, priced quotes, marketplace submissions, public listings. Each authorised by me, gated, logged, revocable. Two rules with no exceptions: it does not sign, and it does not move money.
