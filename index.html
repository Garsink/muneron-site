AI agents, built around your company.

Tell HAI what needs to get done. Working with you, it sets up a routine or an agent for the job: the company context it needs, the sources it may read, the rules it works under and when it runs. You define access and approvals. HAI supervises the work: what ran, what did not, and what needs a person.

  1. 1

    Define the job

    In plain words, from you or proposed by HAI from what it sees in the company.

  2. 2

    Configure around your company

    In a working session with you: company context, the sources it may read, the rules it follows, the tools it may use, the schedule.

  3. 3

    Run within approved access

    Only the sources and actions you granted, each with a stop switch.

  4. 4

    Supervise and surface

    What ran, what did not, what went quiet, and what needs a person, reported to the owner.

Weekly management brief

Reads the week's numbers and open items, writes the brief, flags what changed.

Supplier updates

Checks confirmations and delivery dates against open orders, raises the late ones.

Follow-up on open work

Chases what was promised, records what came back, escalates what did not.

Then it carries the work forward.

A customer asks for a quote on a part your purchasing team last bought eight months ago. Here is what happens between the request and the result.

  1. 1

    Source information

    The request, the ERP record, last year's supplier offer and the customer's open invoice are read together.

  2. 2

    The issue that matters

    The last supplier price is stale, and the customer has an overdue invoice. Both are surfaced, with the source next to each.

  3. 3

    Proposal with an owner

    A draft quote with a refreshed price check goes to the sales owner. The credit question is routed to finance.

  4. 4

    Decision remembered

    Finance approves on terms. The decision is recorded against the customer, with who decided and when.

  5. 5

    Follow-up with evidence

    Three days later: quote sent, acknowledged, order received. Or not, and the owner is told why, with the trail.

Illustrative scenario. The company, figures and names are not from a real customer. Which actions HAI may take itself, and which wait for a person, is set per company.

What HAI is made of.

Six parts, each one a benefit you can point to.

Company context

Your products, customers, suppliers, people and terms, held in one place that HAI reads first. Nobody re-explains the business every morning.

Connections to approved sources

Mail, documents, accounting and the ERP, connected read-only and scoped to what you grant. Your systems stay the systems of record.

Decisions and corrections that persist

What you decide and what you correct is written down and used next time. The company gets more useful the longer it runs.

Tailored agents and routines

Each job gets an agent or a routine configured around your sources, rules and schedule, with shared context and oversight.

Roles and follow-up

Sales, purchasing, finance and management each get their own view, with that role's permissions. Follow-ups are chased, and what could not be done is reported.

Permissions and verification

Every figure shows its source. Outbound actions exist only where you approve them, each behind rules you set and a stop switch.

Built where the stakes were real.

HAI was built inside an aviation parts, MRO and landing gear leasing group, by its owner, while the business ran on it. That internal deployment is the reference implementation, and it is held to account: 219 scheduled jobs on four machines as of 20 September 2026, audited adversarially and re-checked by hand.

Start with one workflow.

Pick one recurring, valuable workflow: customer enquiries, purchasing exceptions, the weekly management picture. Define its sources, its owner and the output you expect. HAI runs it. You expand when it proves useful.

Starter

A portable HAI that runs on a computer in your company, with your own Claude account. It reads what you grant, builds the company context, briefs the owner and proposes where to go next. It does not send, write to your systems or move money.

Connected deployment

Your systems connected, agents and routines configured around your workflows and supervised, roles and permissions set for your team, and the actions you approve put behind rules and a stop switch. Scoped and agreed per company.

Questions owners ask.

Is this another chatbot?
No. A chat answers the question in front of it. HAI is a digital company: it holds the company context, connects approved sources, keeps decisions, runs agents and routines, and follows up. Talking to it is one of its doors.
Do we replace our systems?
No. Your ERP, accounting and mail stay the systems of record. HAI reads them and works alongside them.
Who controls access and actions?
You do. Access is granted per source and per role. Outbound actions exist only where you approve them, each with its own stop switch. HAI does not sign documents or move money.
How do we start?
With one workflow, its sources and its owner. Tell us about it, and we reply with what we would set up first and what we would need from you.
