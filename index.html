The quoting problem, named.

Hundreds of RFQs · minutes to answer · half never answered

The flood is constant.

Email, ILS, PartsBase — every request means checking stock, condition, trace and price before a competitive quote goes out. For an AOG, within minutes. Even well-tooled shops leave nearly half of RFQs unanswered.

Autoquote exists. It's off.

Your ERP probably has the checkbox. Rule-based tools match exact part numbers, miss alternates, and can't be trusted with prices — a wrong quote to a good customer costs more than a slow one. So it stays off.

The gap is trust, not tech.

The distance between owning automation and trusting it is where deals are lost. Closing that gap — with guards, approval lanes and measured outcomes — is exactly what MUNERON does.

Three commitments.

Aviation businesses are right to be cautious about AI. These are the rules we run on — the same ones the platform we built for ourselves runs on every day.

01 · Control

A human approves everything that leaves.

Draft-not-send is the default on every lane. Every action logged, every lane with a kill switch. The system never signs and never moves money. Autonomy is earned, workflow by workflow — never assumed.

02 · Price

Prices are published and identical for everyone.

No custom quotes, no discovery-call pricing dance. The price list on this page is the price. Staged payments and a week-two stop clause protect you, not us.

03 · Proof

Outcomes are measured, monthly, in your numbers.

Quotes sent, hours saved, bad prices caught, RFQs no longer ignored. If the numbers stop earning the fee, you should fire us — and you’ll have the report to decide with.

Why this matters: most AI projects fail quietly — a wrong price sent to a good customer, a job that reports success while doing nothing. We made those mistakes on our own company first. The guardrails above are what came out of them.
