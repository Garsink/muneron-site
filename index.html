Deividas Jurkštas

I run a company on agents I built myself.

Aviation parts, MRO and landing gear leasing. I am not a developer and I did not write the code. I described what I wanted, then I checked it, for more than a year.

Plenty of people write about AI agents. Very few have had to live with two hundred of them running their own money. This page is the record: what mine did, and what they got wrong.

Tell me what you run ↗

The record.

  1. 218agents loaded across four machines. Counted today, not quoted from a slide.
  2. 311of their jobs read end to end in one audit, on 2 September.
  3. 102finished clean, showed green, and were either wrong or reached nobody. Not one had thrown an error.
  4. 20of those 102, picked at random, re-verified by hand against the original logs. Nineteen held. One did not.

The one I got wrong.

A vendor who never shows you their misses is showing you a brochure.

  1. What the audit said

    A daily cash alert was never reaching me.

  2. What it read

    Two lines in the log, both saying blocked.

  3. What the very next line said

    Sent. 308 milliseconds later, in the same file. Across the full log, 108 of those 132 alerts went out.

The finding was wrong, and it was wrong in the way that matters: it read a step in the reasoning as if it were the result. So it is published, and the rule that would have caught it is now in the checker.

If you are running this too, I would like to hear what broke.

No deck, no proposal. Tell me what you run and what you cannot account for.
